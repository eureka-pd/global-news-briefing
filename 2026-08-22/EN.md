### 6. Rust `arrayref` supply-chain attack; malicious 0.3.10 lived 86 minutes on crates.io

- **Source/time:** GeekNews curation 10:33:24 KST on 2026-08-21; Rust security-response blog dated 2026-08-20 — [GeekNews](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=32715) · [Rust blog](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2026/08/20/supply-chain-attack-on-arrayref/)
- **Source facts:** The Rust Security Response Team said it received a report at 07:15 UTC on 2026-08-20 that `proc-macro1` was malicious, and verified a build script downloading a remote payload. `proc-macro1` and `proc-macro-en`, `aovine`, `arone`, `aronenao` and `tinymember` were deleted. Popular crate `arrayref` had been republished to depend on it, with recent versions yanked. The team removed the malicious version, unyanked the yanked releases, did the same for `internment` and `append-only-vec`, and locked the account as a precaution. It does not believe the `arrayref` author acted maliciously; their machine or credentials are likely compromised. Deleted malicious versions: `arrayref@0.3.10` published 07:15:00Z, deleted 08:41:40Z (86 minutes); `internment@0.8.7` 90 minutes; `append-only-vec@0.1.9` 107 minutes. Initial discovery: Nextron Systems research team. GeekNews said 0.3.10 added typosquat `proc-macro1` so a compile alone ran the payload, abusing Cargo’s build of unused non-optional dependencies, over TLS that skipped certificate checks, writing `/tmp/rust-setup` or Windows `%TEMP%`. Lifetime downloads of about 245 million measure reach, not infections, it said.
- **Analysis:** The official blog timestamp (20 August UTC) may sit just before the window. GeekNews curation is inside. Download counts are not infected builds. Account compromise is the team’s inference. Deleting from crates.io does not purge already-cached `.crate` files.
