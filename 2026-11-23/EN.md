# 2026-11-23 Global News Briefing

Coverage: 2026-11-22 04:00–2026-11-23 04:00 KST (2026-11-21 19:00–2026-11-22 19:00 UTC)

## 1. South Korea prepares standby equity support
**Fact:** At the Nov. 22 emergency F4 meeting, authorities began preparing to activate the stock-market stabilization fund if needed. They plan to ease listed firms’ daily buyback-order limits for three months from Nov. 24 and intensify monitoring of illegal short selling for three months. [1]

**Context:** This came ahead of Seoul’s reopening after a U.S. selloff. The fund has not been deployed, and the monitoring is not a blanket ban on legal short selling.

## 2. Chinese exchanges lower the minimum financing margin
**Fact:** With regulator approval, the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing exchanges will cut the minimum margin ratio for buying securities on credit from 100% to 80%, effective Nov. 30. [2]

**Context:** Unlike Korea’s buyback measure, China’s change eases the financing requirement for investors. It is not a new index close or evidence of actual inflows.

## 3. COP31 closes without an overarching agreement
**Fact:** The extended Antalya summit ended without an overarching decision. The omission of language on moving away from fossil fuels and differences on climate finance contributed to the impasse. [3]

**Context:** The result does not cancel existing Paris Agreement commitments or mean that every individual agenda item failed.

## 4. Russian vessel struck by explosion in Benghazi, Libya
**Fact:** Libya’s eastern authorities said a preliminary investigation points to a suspected drone strike after an explosion on a Russian ship at the Benghazi naval base. They did not identify the attacker. [4]

**Context:** The incident highlights risks to Russian vessels in the Mediterranean, but does not establish responsibility.

## 5. Microsoft highlights the trade-off between patch speed and testing
**Fact:** Microsoft president Brad Smith told CBS on Nov. 22 that AI can accelerate vulnerability discovery and fixes, but cutting testing time around changes to older systems can raise the risk of disruption. Smith said Microsoft’s own recent outage was a technical failure unrelated to AI. [5]

**Context:** This was an operational warning, not a new product or policy. It does not show that any specific outage was an AI attack.
