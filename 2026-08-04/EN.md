## Key Summary

### 1. Six AI-generated SQLite vulnerability claims registered as critical CVEs

- **Source and time:** GeekNews, 2026-08-03 22:32 KST — [GeekNews summary](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=32105), [JFrog original investigation](https://research.jfrog.com/post/sqlite-critical-cves-or-llm-slops/)
- **Source facts:** According to a JFrog investigation introduced by GeekNews, six critical CVEs related to SQLite did not correspond to actual code or reproducible behavior and could not be reproduced in the investigation team's validation. JFrog highlighted the problem of vulnerability reports apparently produced by generative AI entering the CVE system without sufficient technical verification.
- **Analysis:** As AI-based security automation grows, reproducibility procedures and human responsibility for verification matter more than detection volume. Incorrect CVEs can distort patch prioritization and supply-chain risk assessment.

### 2. EU AI Act transparency obligations for generated content and AI interactions take effect

- **Source and time:** AI Times, 2026-08-03 18:28 KST — [AI Times](https://www.aitimes.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=41297), [European Commission guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-guidelines-transparency-obligations-providers-and-deployers-certain-ai-systems)
- **Source facts:** AI Times reported that, under the EU AI Act, transparency obligations began applying from August 2: users must be informed when they are interacting with AI systems such as chatbots, and certain AI-generated or manipulated content and deepfakes must be labelled. The Commission also published related guidance.
- **Analysis:** AI providers and deployers serving EU users need to turn on-screen notices, synthetic-media labelling, and content-provenance records into operational procedures. This is a change that directly affects product compliance design more than model performance.

### 3. Construction begins on the National AI Computing Center in Haenam, South Jeolla

### 4. Global oil prices plunge after the U.S. announces it will hold off on additional strikes against Iran

- **Source and time:** AP, 2026-08-03 08:02 KST (2026-08-02 23:02 UTC) — [AP](https://apnews.com/article/194ab3d3130bb44de445cf3d05954beb)
- **Source facts:** AP reported that global oil prices fell sharply after U.S. President Donald Trump said he would hold off on new strikes against Iran while citing the possibility of negotiations. The announcement at the time concerned a pause in attacks and the prospect of talks; it was not a confirmed peace agreement.
- **Analysis:** Oil prices, which are sensitive to risks around the Strait of Hormuz and Middle Eastern supply, reacted immediately to the diplomatic signal. For South Korea, which depends heavily on energy imports, this is a short-term easing factor for inflation, the won, and corporate costs, but its durability is difficult to assert because negotiations could reverse.
