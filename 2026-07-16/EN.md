## Verified facts

### Technology

1. **JetBrains released Hexana tooling for WebAssembly and binary inspection.**
   - GeekNews featured [Hexana](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=31445) at 09:31 on July 15. Its JetBrains plugin and VS Code extension share a Kotlin Multiplatform core and inspect, edit, run and debug WebAssembly; ELF, Mach-O and PE analysis and breakpoint debugging are labeled experimental.

2. **PostHog published a company case for an AI-assisted SQL-parser rewrite.**
   - GeekNews highlighted [PostHog's account](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=31457) at 11:19. PostHog says multiple Claude Code sessions produced a 16,000-line Rust parser plus tooling and tests, with about 70 times faster laptop performance and 454 times faster production averages. These are company-reported results; equivalence was tested against the old parser with property tests, anonymized queries and shadow traffic.

3. **A security researcher disclosed a repository-triggered execution issue in Cursor for Windows.**
   - GeekNews posted the [Mindgard disclosure](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=31461) at 21:35. The researcher says Cursor could execute a malicious `git.exe` placed at a repository root merely when the project was opened, under the user's privileges. The last cited validation was Cursor 3.2.16 on April 30; current patch status was not independently established here.

4. **A single-system test found sub-millisecond X11-versus-Wayland differences but a larger XWayland penalty.**
   - At 00:36 on July 16, GeekNews posted a [500 Hz click-to-photon measurement](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=31465). X11 with VRR and `dxvk-low-latency` was only 0.72 ms lower in median end-to-end latency than baseline Wayland, while XWayland added up to 3.13 ms. The author cautions that the result is hardware-, software- and workload-specific.

5. **Oracle added an AI-native builder for multi-agent Fusion applications.**
   - AI Times reported at 22:14 on [Oracle AI Agent Studio for Fusion Applications](https://www.aitimes.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=41006). Oracle says no-code, low-code and professional developers can build agent teams inside existing Fusion security, approval, workflow and audit controls, with VS Code, CLI, Git, Codex and Claude Code integration. Operational gains remain vendor expectations.

6. **Anthropic launched a free premium Claude service for U.S. K–12 teachers.**
   - AI Times reported at 21:57 on [Claude for Teachers](https://www.aitimes.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=41005). The service connects to standards from all 50 states and education tools for lesson planning, differentiated materials and assessment. The reported workload benefits and educational outcomes have not yet been independently established.

7. **Three Korean companies announced a commercial “full-stack sovereign AI” deployment.**
   - AI Times reported at 11:49 that [FuriosaAI's RNGD NPU, Upstage's Solar model and Daum's AI Summary service](https://www.aitimes.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=40992) are being connected. The companies say three nodes process 500 million tokens per day and cut inference cost by more than 50%; the beta currently handles about 20% of Daum queries. Those performance and cost figures are company claims.

### Markets and economy

1. **Korea added 63,000 jobs in June, but its employment rate fell for a third straight month.**
   - KBS World reported at 10:29 that [employment reached 29.15 million](https://world.kbs.co.kr/service/news_view.htm?Seq_Code=202903&lang=e). The employment rate fell 0.2 percentage point year over year to 63.4%, while youth employment dropped 1.7 points to 43.9%, its 26th consecutive monthly decline.

2. **Korea set its 2027 minimum wage at 10,700 won an hour.**
   - KBS World reported at 09:18 that the [tripartite commission approved a 3.7% increase](https://world.kbs.co.kr/service/news_view.htm?Seq_Code=202901&lang=e) from 10,320 won. The figure emerged after 12 adjustment rounds and a vote of 27 commission members; labor and business representatives both expressed reservations.

3. **The KOSPI rebounded 6.24% as technology shares recovered.**
   - KBS World reported at 17:07 that the [KOSPI closed up 427.58 points at 7,284.41](https://world.kbs.co.kr/service/news_view.htm?Seq_Code=202921&lang=e). The KOSDAQ gained 5.80% to 829.43, and the won strengthened to 1,484.7 per dollar at 15:30.

4. **Korea presented a second-half agenda covering inflation, the won and strategic investment.**
   - The Korea Times reported at 15:46 on the government's [10-point economic agenda](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/economy/policy/20260715/korea-to-keep-inflation-below-3-accelerate-won-internationalization). It targets second-half consumer inflation below 3%, phased offshore access to won trading, a new state-assets law and an expansion of the National Growth Fund from 150 trillion to 200 trillion won. These are policy plans, not completed outcomes.

5. **China's second-quarter growth slowed to 4.3%, the weakest in more than three years.**
   - AP published at 11:16 on July 15 that [growth fell from 5% in the first quarter](https://apnews.com/article/china-economy-trade-exports-ai-95136222f87d5a1e62918f41efab00be). First-half exports rose 17.6%, but fixed-asset investment fell 5.7% and retail sales rose only 1.3%, underscoring the gap between export manufacturing and domestic demand.

6. **World shares were broadly flat as chip earnings offset renewed oil and Iran risk.**
   - A [Reuters market snapshot published at 20:59 KST](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/stocks-steady-oil-surge-offsets-asml-lift-tech) put the MSCI world index broadly unchanged and the STOXX 600 just below flat at 11:42 GMT. Nasdaq futures were up 0.5%, S&P 500 futures 0.1%, and ASML about 4%. These were intraday figures, not U.S. closing levels.

- **AI deployment is moving toward integrated execution layers, but verification and security are lagging.** Enterprise agents, education services and sovereign stacks are expanding, while the Cursor disclosure and company-reported benchmarks show why patch status, independent tests and governance controls matter.
- **Korea's market rebound does not erase labor softness.** A sharp chip-led equity recovery and larger strategic funds sit alongside a falling employment rate and a 26-month decline in youth employment.
- **Global growth is split between high-tech exports and weak domestic demand.** ASML and AI-linked markets were resilient, but China's consumption and investment data show that export strength alone is not a balanced recovery.
- **Geopolitical risk is increasingly transmitted through energy, sanctions and industrial capacity.** Hormuz disruption, Russia-focused secondary tariffs and joint weapons production all connect security decisions directly to prices, trade and fiscal commitments.
