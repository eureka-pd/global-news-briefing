## 3. Moonshot announces cyber-risk restrictions in Kimi K3.1 Pro

**Facts:** Moonshot introduced Kimi K3.1 Pro on October 24 and said it removed cyber-related material from training data and reinforcement-learning environments. Model weights are scheduled for release **by November 4**, not already released. Its internal cyber benchmark fell to 81.0%, compared with 90.5% for a similar unrestricted model; Reuters said these results were not independently verified.

**Analysis:** The announcement combines open-weight model competition with misuse-risk management. Company benchmarks do not establish independently verified superiority or guarantee safety.

**Source/time:** [Reuters, Eduardo Baptista](https://www.reuters.com/technology/moonshot-curbs-hacking-ability-new-kimi-model-fears-grow-over-ai-powered-2026-10-24/), October 24, 13:25 GMT+9. The update label read “13 hours ago” when viewed, before cutoff; exact minutes and seconds were unavailable.

## 4. UK announces approximately £600 million in AI security investment

**Facts:** Reuters reported that the UK government announced plans on Saturday for approximately £600 million ($800 million). Components include £100 million for an AI biosecurity laboratory, £65 million for pathogen countermeasures, an additional £120 million for the AI Security Institute and £130 million for cyber defence. Planned model credits worth up to £200 million from OpenAI and Anthropic are distinct from government cash spending.

**Analysis:** The plan builds biological and cyber-risk infrastructure alongside AI adoption. Announced plans are not completed disbursements, and the listed components are not an exhaustive budget breakdown.

**Source/time:** [Reuters, David Milliken](https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/uk-government-sets-out-800-million-ai-security-funding-2026-10-23/), October 24, 08:03 GMT+9, with an update label of “20 hours ago.” [Euronext's Reuters distribution](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/uk-government-sets-out-800-million-ai-security-funding) displayed October 24, 01:03 without a timezone. Article timestamps, not the URL date, determined inclusion. The government release was not obtained.
