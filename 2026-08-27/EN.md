- **Source facts:** Reuters said Meta agreed to pay a maximum $16.68 billion and change Facebook and Instagram nationwide — including daily usage limits and nighttime blocks for teens — to resolve claims by 29 states that it designed the apps to addict children, misled consumers about safety and improperly collected children’s data. The deal was reached during a California federal trial; Meta denied wrongdoing. The 29 states also alleged a COPPA violation for collecting data from users it knew were children without parental notice or consent and using that data to train machine-learning and generative-AI models. Before trial Meta said California, Colorado, Kentucky and New Jersey were seeking up to $1.4 trillion; the states put the figure closer to $200 billion. Reuters noted New Mexico’s March jury award of $375 million and an August 6 public-nuisance order for a further $567 million plus youth-safety measures, and a March Los Angeles verdict of $6 million combined against Meta and Google for plaintiff Kaley G.M., which the companies said they would appeal.
- **Analysis:** $16.68 billion is a cap, not a locked cash outlay. Reuters did not specify the hour-length of the daily/night limits. Court approval may still be required. Yahoo’s +4.4% pre-market print is not Wednesday’s regular close, which sits outside this window. reuters.com returned an empty body here; figures were checked on the Yahoo reprint.
