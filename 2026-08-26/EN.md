### 3. Canada details 15–50% counter-tariffs on about $27.6 billion / ~700 U.S. product lines from 8 September

- **Source/time:** Finance Canada official list (effective 8 September); BNN Bloomberg published 05:01 EDT / updated 12:32 EDT on 2026-08-25 — [Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/international-trade-finance-policy/canadas-response-us-tariffs/complete-list-us-products-subject-to-counter-tariffs.html) · [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/tariffs/2026/08/25/from-makeup-to-smartphones-to-kitchen-appliances-canada-outlines-us-counter-tariffs/)
- **Source facts:** Ottawa said that after the U.S. imposed a 50% tariff on $27.6 billion of Canadian goods from 22 August, Canada will match dollar-for-dollar with countermeasures on $27.6 billion of U.S. imports from 12:01 a.m. on 8 September. The official page concentrates steel and aluminum, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, pulp and paper, plastics and electronics, and raises some steel/aluminum counters from 25% to 50%; existing auto counters remain. BNN said about 700 U.S. product lines at 15, 25 or 50%, matched to the U.S. rate on the same goods—50% examples include perfume/makeup, smartphones, milk products, tableware, plywood/paper, honey/molasses, doors/windows and cutlery; 25% seafood, large appliances, cheese, carpets; 15% air-conditioning machines. Energy and potash are not on the list. Ministers also outlined a about $7.5 billion support package (temporary EI changes and other liquidity). Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne called U.S. terms “uneconomic, unfair, and ultimately unacceptable.”
- **Analysis:** $27.6 billion is the official Canadian figure; earlier wires that used about $20 billion are a different source line. Canada.ca’s HS table is the authoritative schedule. Joly’s “target states” remark is political intent, not a state-by-state rate card. Officials did not publish a consumer-cost total.
